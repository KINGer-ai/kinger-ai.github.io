AI-supported activity One-sentence exhibit summary

AI rewrites a museum label into one clear sentence before the visitor reads the full text.

Context XJTLU Museum labels

The Museum presents university history and cultural exhibitions to a diverse campus audience.

Participants 20 students or staff

Each participant completes a short online session lasting around 10 minutes.

Design Within-subjects experiment

Every participant reads original and AI-summary labels for three matched exhibit themes.

Museum labels can contain names, dates, and cultural context that are difficult to understand quickly. A one-sentence AI summary may help visitors identify the main idea before deciding whether to read the full label. However, the summary may also omit important detail or change the meaning of the original text.

The experiment is deliberately small. It does not try to measure the complete museum experience, compare several AI systems, or test long-term learning. It tests one practical design option that could be added to a label without changing the physical exhibition. Participants only need to read short texts and answer simple questions, so the study can be completed on a laptop or phone.

The experiment asks a small practical question: does one AI sentence help visitors understand the exhibit more accurately and easily?

A useful result should also be actionable. If the AI sentence improves both accuracy and perceived ease, the group can recommend a small Museum pilot. If it improves only perceived ease, the recommendation should be cautious because the label may feel clearer without helping visitors understand the important content. This distinction keeps the project practical and prevents the study from treating a positive satisfaction rating as proof of learning.

02 · Problem and motivation

Problem, motivation, and research questions

Visitors do not read museum labels in the same way. Some move quickly through an exhibition, while others stop for detailed information. A short summary can support the first group without preventing the second group from reading the original label. This makes the one-sentence summary a practical design idea for XJTLU Museum.

The idea also fits the Museum's mixed audience. Students and staff may have different levels of familiarity with art, history, or the cultural context of an exhibition. A plain-language first line can provide an entry point without removing the detailed curatorial text. If the summary improves understanding, it can be tested in a real exhibition; if it does not, the Museum avoids adding unnecessary text to every label.

The design question is also easy to test. The study can compare two versions of the same exhibit label: the original text and an AI-generated one-sentence summary placed above it. The experiment measures whether visitors answer comprehension questions more accurately and rate the label as easier to understand.

Research questions

RQ1 · Comprehension

Does an AI one-sentence summary improve accuracy on an exhibit comprehension question?

RQ2 · Perceived ease

Does the summary make the label feel easier to understand?

Hypotheses

  • H1: Comprehension accuracy will be higher when the AI summary is present.
  • H2: Participants will rate the label as easier to understand when the AI summary is present.

These questions keep the study focused. They do not attempt to measure long-term learning, cultural appreciation, trust in AI, or the overall visitor experience.

03 · Background and related work

Background and related work

Cognitive load theory explains that readers have limited working memory. A label filled with names and dates can make it harder to identify the main message [1]. A short summary can act as an advance organiser: it gives the visitor a simple idea before the detailed text. This may improve comprehension and reduce effort.

Museum interpretation research treats labels as communication tools. A strong label does more than list facts; it helps visitors notice what matters and connect it to what they already know [2]. This supports the use of a short summary as an interpretation aid.

Human-AI interaction guidelines also matter. If AI rewrites curatorial text, the system should avoid presenting uncertain interpretations as certain facts [3]. The experiment therefore keeps the original label visible and tests the summary as an additional aid, not as a replacement.

Research gap: AI summaries are easy to generate, but there is no simple local test showing whether they help campus visitors understand an XJTLU Museum label.

The study also responds to a practical limitation in museum evaluation. Large visitor surveys are expensive and require long recruitment periods. A controlled reading experiment is faster and more focused. It can show whether the core design idea is promising before the group asks Museum staff or visitors to engage with a more complex evaluation.

04 · System and evaluation design

System and evaluation design

Materials

Three short exhibit texts will be adapted from public XJTLU Museum themes: university history, cultural exchange, and visual art. The texts will be de-identified and will not include copyrighted images. Each theme has an original-style label and a version with an AI-generated one-sentence summary at the top. The critical facts remain the same in both versions.

A member of the group generates the summaries with XIPU AI using a fixed prompt. The group then checks each summary against the original label to ensure that no important information has been removed. The experiment measures the effect of the summary, not the quality of the AI interface.

Participants and sampling

The study will recruit 20 XJTLU students or staff aged 18 or above. A within-subjects design is used because each participant can compare both versions of the same exhibit. This reduces the influence of individual reading speed and lowers the number of participants needed compared with separate groups.

Recruitment will use approved university channels and a short sign-up form. Participants do not need to visit the Museum or use XIPU AI during the session, so data collection can be completed online. Participation is anonymous and takes approximately 10 minutes.

The sampling method is convenience sampling, which is appropriate for a course experiment but limits generalisation. The group will record how participants were recruited and whether they have previously visited the Museum. This information will be reported as descriptive context, not used to make claims about the wider public.

All participants will receive the same instructions and the same number of reading items. This keeps the session short and reduces the chance that fatigue or missing data will affect one condition more than the other.

Ethics and bias control

Data collection will begin after ethics approval. Consent will explain anonymity, voluntary participation, and withdrawal. The materials use public exhibition themes and do not contain personal records, museum-confidential documents, or assessment data. The group will run a pilot with two participants and revise ambiguous questions before formal collection.

05 · Data preparation and analysis

Data preparation and analysis

Data will be exported from the online survey platform as a CSV file and checked for incomplete sessions. Each participant will have paired values for the original and AI-summary conditions. The analysis will calculate means, standard deviations, and differences for comprehension accuracy and perceived ease.

Because accuracy is binary at the item level, the participant-level accuracy percentage will be used for comparison. Paired-samples t-tests will be used if the difference scores are approximately normal. If not, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests will be used. The report will include exact p-values, effect sizes, and confidence intervals where appropriate.

Reading time will be reported descriptively and used as supporting evidence. Extreme times will be inspected but not removed unless a documented technical error makes the response invalid. The analysis will remain simple and transparent because the sample is small.

The outcome data can be prepared quickly. Each participant will have six short answer rows and paired summary columns for accuracy and ease. The group will create a codebook before analysis, label every variable, and retain the original anonymous export. This makes the analysis reproducible and provides the required supplementary evidence without exposing participant identities.

07 · Discussion and recommendations

Discussion, limitations, and recommendations

The study will show whether a simple AI summary adds practical value to a museum label. If comprehension and perceived ease both improve, XJTLU Museum could test AI summaries as an optional first line above the original text. If ease improves but comprehension does not, the summary may create a feeling of clarity without supporting accurate understanding.

A third possibility is that the summary has little effect. This result would also be useful. It would suggest that the original labels are already clear for this participant group, or that the summary style did not address the real source of difficulty. The discussion will report this outcome rather than treating a non-significant result as a failure.

The final recommendation will depend on the pattern of evidence. If accuracy improves and ease improves, the group can propose an optional AI summary with curatorial review. If only ease improves, the Museum should revise the summary and test it again. If neither outcome improves, the group should recommend keeping the original label and investigating other visitor barriers, such as font size, placement, language, or exhibition flow.

Limitations

  • The experiment uses three short texts and does not represent the full Museum programme.
  • The sample is small and may not represent all campus visitors.
  • The online session does not reproduce the physical museum environment.
  • The study measures immediate comprehension, not long-term learning.
  • Results depend on the prompt and writing style used for the AI summary.

Recommendations

  1. Keep the original museum label available even when an AI summary is added.
  2. Ask curatorial staff to check every AI-generated summary before use.
  3. Make clear that the short line is AI-assisted rather than the original curatorial voice.
  4. Run a follow-up test with multilingual visitors and more exhibits before permanent adoption.
