02 · Research question

Overall project question and embedded experiment

Overall project focus

How usable is the XJTLU Museum AI system for visitors, and how does AI-supported guidance shape their museum experience?

Embedded experiment question

How do post-visit experience ratings differ between students who used the museum's AI guide and students who toured without it?

The embedded questionnaire measured nine related dimensions rather than one global score. This sub-study does not replace the wider usability evaluation. Its analysis contains no verified pre-specified primary endpoint, so each item is treated as exploratory.

Analysis boundary: the study compares self-reported post-visit ratings. It does not measure task success, completion time, SUS, learning with a knowledge test, or behavioural use of the AI guide. A later live-form Q16 on AI-guide smoothness was not present in the final dataset and is excluded from this analysis.

Data verification: the linked Wenjuanxing dashboard now shows 30 complete responses. Q1 and Q2 each have 30 valid answers, Q3 is balanced at 15 AI and 15 no-AI responses, and every Q7–Q15 item has 30 responses. These counts match the cleaned analysis file.

Q7–Q15 group comparison.

This embedded dataset contains 15 participants in the AI-guide group and 15 in the no-AI comparison group. Both groups reported visiting the same exhibition. The questionnaire also records student level, previous museum visits, and general AI-tool frequency as context.

Allocation caution: the team planned to alternate groups by arrival order, but the recorded group sequence is not strictly alternating. The report therefore does not call the study randomized and does not interpret the group comparison as a causal effect of the AI guide.

04 · Data and statistical method

From questionnaire export to nine exploratory tests

30Unique participant IDs
15 + 15AI guide / no AI guide
270 / 270Valid item ratings
9Holm-adjusted comparisons

The clean CSV maps the categorical answers to numeric codes without changing the Q7–Q15 scores. Checks found 30 unique IDs, no duplicate records, complete consent and recent-visit confirmation, and no missing or out-of-range experience ratings.

Because each item is ordinal, the analysis uses a two-sided independent-samples Mann–Whitney U test with the no-AI group as the reference group. Holm correction is applied across the nine related tests. Rank-biserial correlation describes effect direction and magnitude: positive values favour the AI group and negative values favour the no-AI group. Means are descriptive; the tests use ranks.

Why no SUS or task-success test: the final questionnaire and cleaned dataset do not contain those variables. Reusing the old usability analysis would create results that the data cannot support.

Strongest mean difference

Q9: +0.47 favouring the AI group, raw p = .230, Holm p = 1.000.

No item reached the two-sided .05 threshold before correction, and all nine Holm-adjusted p-values remained 1.000. All rank-biserial effect magnitudes were below .30. The result is absence of evidence for a difference in this sample, not evidence that the conditions are equivalent.

06 · Interpretation and limits

A cautious answer and a stronger next study

The current data do not provide sufficient evidence that using the museum AI guide changes any of the nine post-visit experience ratings. The Q9 learning difference is the largest descriptive signal, but it is not statistically reliable after accounting for sampling variation and multiple comparisons.

Limitations

  • The recorded group sequence does not match strict arrival-order alternation, so causal attribution is unjustified.
  • The sample contains 30 students from one museum context and may not represent other visitors.
  • All outcomes are post-visit self-reports; the study contains no behavioural or knowledge test.
  • Nine exploratory comparisons were available without a verified pre-specified primary endpoint.
  • With 15 participants per group, the analysis has limited power to detect modest differences.

What the next study should change

  1. Verify and document the allocation process before data collection.
  2. Pre-specify one primary experience outcome and retain the others as secondary measures.
  3. Recruit a larger sample and keep the exhibition route and timing comparable across groups.
  4. Add objective outcomes only after confirming a clear research question and ethical approval.
