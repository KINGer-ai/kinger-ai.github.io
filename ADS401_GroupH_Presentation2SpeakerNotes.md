# ADS401 Presentation 2 - 3-Minute Speaker Notes

## Slide 1 - Title (15 seconds)

Good morning. We are Group H, and today we present our evaluation design for the XJTLU Museum AI System. Our study asks whether visitors can use the system to complete a short museum tour without confusion or unnecessary support.

## Slide 2 - Problem and research questions (30 seconds)

The practical problem is that a public-facing AI guide must work without a manual. Visitors may not know how to start, what to ask, where to go next, or how to recover from an unclear response. We focus on three questions: can visitors complete the core tasks, how usable and efficient is the system, and which barriers recur. Our target users are XJTLU students and staff with different levels of digital confidence.

## Slide 3 - Study design (35 seconds)

We use a moderated task-based usability study. Each participant completes four tasks: start the guide, ask an exhibit question, move to the next stop, and recover from an unclear answer. The tasks follow the visitor journey and let us observe where the interaction breaks down. We will recruit 12 to 15 participants, in line with the module guidance. We combine observed behaviour with think-aloud comments rather than relying only on opinions.

## Slide 4 - Procedure and materials (30 seconds)

The session lasts about 15 minutes. Participants receive an information sheet and consent form, complete a short warm-up, carry out the four tasks, complete the 10-question bilingual Wenjuanxing survey, and answer a few exit questions. The live participant link is shown on the slide. We also prepared a moderator script, task cards, an observation sheet, and a timer. We do not collect passwords, photographs, or personal museum records.

## Slide 5 - Measures and analysis (35 seconds)

We record task success as independent, assisted, or failed. We also record time, errors, and the point where assistance is needed. SUS provides a global usability score, while the 1-to-7 ratings measure task ease and confidence. H1 predicts at least eighty percent independent success, H2 predicts SUS of at least sixty-eight, H3 predicts positive associations between ease, confidence, and SUS, and H4 predicts recurring onboarding, navigation, clarity, or recovery barriers.

## Slide 6 - Implementation status (25 seconds)

The live Wenjuanxing survey and participant materials are ready. Before recruitment, we need to confirm access to the current Museum AI version and prepare a fallback walkthrough in case of technical problems. We will first run a two-person pilot to test timing, task wording, and the observation sheet. After that, we will run the main sessions. We have not yet completed participant data collection, so we are not presenting invented findings.

## Slide 7 - Closing (10 seconds)

The final output will be a task success profile, a usability score, a clear set of barriers, and prioritised recommendations for the Museum AI interface. Thank you. We are happy to take questions about the design, measures, or implementation plan.

## Likely Q&A

- Why 12 to 15 participants? It meets the module usability guidance and is enough to identify repeated interaction problems.
- Why no control condition? The study evaluates one existing system. The research questions concern usability and barriers, not a comparison between systems.
- Why measure trust? AI information can sound confident even when it is incomplete. Trust shows whether visitors understand the system's limits.
- What if the system is unavailable? We will request approved access or use a recorded walkthrough or approved prototype, and we will report the change.
- What are the limitations? The sample is small, the setting is controlled, and the study covers selected tasks and one system version.
- Is the sample data real? Not yet. The slides present the implementation plan and materials. Actual pilot and main-session data must be collected and added before the final presentation.
